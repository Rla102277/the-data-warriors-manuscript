[RADICALLY SIMPLIFIED VERSION]

**WHAT DATA LITERACY ACTUALLY MEANS**

Forget the textbook definition. Here's the real question: Can someone in your organization look at a chart and tell if it's bullshit?

Not "do they know SQL"—can they ask the right questions? Can they spot when data is being used to support a predetermined conclusion? Can they tell the difference between correlation and causation?

That's data literacy. And it exists on a spectrum:

**Level 1 - Data Citizens**: Can read a chart without panicking. Understands that "correlation doesn't imply causation" even if they can't define correlation. Knows when to be skeptical.

**Level 2 - Data Users**: Can navigate a dashboard, filter data, create basic visualizations. Asks good questions. Knows what they don't know.

**Level 3 - Data Analysts**: You. Can perform analysis, build models, generate insights. But remember—you're not smarter than Level 1 or 2. You just have different tools.

[CUT ALL THE DETAILED GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORKS]

**THE GOVERNANCE MINIMUM**

You don't need a comprehensive data governance platform. You need three things:

1. **Someone owns each dataset** (and can tell you if it's accurate)
2. **Clear rules about sensitive data** (and people actually follow them)  
3. **A way to find data** (so you're not the only one who knows where things are)

Everything else is nice to have. Start with these three.

[PRACTICAL EXERCISE - ONE GOOD ONE INSTEAD OF TEN]

**The Data Literacy Audit**

This week, sit in on three meetings where data is presented. Don't present—just observe. Ask yourself:
- Did people understand the analysis?
- Did they ask good questions?  
- Were there obvious misinterpretations that nobody caught?
- Did the data change anyone's mind, or did they just use it to justify what they already believed?

This will tell you more about your organization's data literacy than any survey.
