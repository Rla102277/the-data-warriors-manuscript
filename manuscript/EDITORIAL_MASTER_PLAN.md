# The Data Warrior's Manuscript — editorial master plan

**Status:** working editorial architecture, not author-approved final manuscript. **Source base:** 20 verified DOCX originals, 4,750 indexed source sections, plus December 2025 recovered chat prose already in this repository. Do not import duplicate Word binaries. Preserve the original archive outside this editorial branch.

## The central argument

Data leadership is not mastery of tools. It is the disciplined practice of understanding terrain, choosing consequential problems, building trustworthy information, adapting the approach, winning organizational adoption, and exercising judgment under uncertainty. The sword is not the warrior; the technology is not the strategy.

## Narrative progression

**Earth (1–5):** establish why the Way matters; study business terrain; define outcomes and constraints; establish trust and stewardship; choose strategy before platform.

**Water (6–9):** adapt method to context; architect for change; move data reliably; learn and unlearn without chasing novelty.

**Fire (10–14):** decide when to engage; draw a Battle Map; execute under pressure; make tradeoffs explicit; stop or retreat when the evidence demands it.

**Wind (15–18):** understand other schools, rivals, assumptions and organizational resistance. Critique techniques without caricaturing practitioners.

**Void (19–22):** perceive omissions and uncertainty; exercise judgment beyond metrics; distinguish experienced intuition from untested confidence; lead responsibly with AI.

**People (23–26):** practice leadership as service; deploy the Council roles as distinct analytical perspectives rather than authorities; resolve conflict; develop successors.

**Tools (27–31):** tools serve the mission; establish engineering discipline; introduce agents and skills as governed assistants; embed governance in delivery; build a living system.

**Practice (32–36):** apply Sword Drills to actual decisions; maintain a Living Scroll; move from Battle Map to measurable outcome; keep a Chronicle; return to the Way.

## Every chapter must earn its place

1. Open with a concrete tension, decision or author-verified experience; no generic samurai spectacle.
2. State one governing principle and its failure mode.
3. Use the strongest available source passage with provenance, while editing for clarity and continuity.
4. Show the principle in a specific, bounded data/AI leadership situation. Label composite or illustrative examples.
5. Close with a Sword Drill that yields an observable artifact or decision and an examiner's question.
6. Avoid repeated prefatory explanations of Musashi, empty aphorisms, unverifiable numerical claims and unsupported quotations.

## Source hierarchy and verification

- **Tier 1:** author-provided verbatim anecdotes, where identifiable; retain original wording in provenance and obtain author confirmation of details.
- **Tier 2:** long-form DOCX narrative and substantive frameworks; compare alternative versions before choosing.
- **Tier 3:** December 2025 recovered chapters; useful prose and scenarios but may contain invented specifics; verify before adoption.
- **Tier 4:** newly written connective tissue and examples, clearly marked as editorial composition.
- Do not promote a document named 'Final' simply because of its filename. Select passages for substance, clarity, credibility and fit.
- The 120K-word long source includes repetitions; length alone is not a quality signal.
- Fact-check Musashi translations, statistics, dates, named clients and claimed business results before publication.

## Production acceptance

36 distinct chapters with consistent narrative voice, source attribution notes, no repeated case study unless intentionally revisited, meaningful Sword Drills, an introduction and conclusion, plus a complete claim/quotation audit. Author approval remains required.
